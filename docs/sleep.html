Picture it

One night, four stages, on repeat

Sleep starts with non-REM sleep p. 10:

  1. Stage 1, light: noises wake you easily p. 10.
  2. Stage 2: slower brain waves p. 10.
  3. Stage 3, deep sleep: very hard to wake from p. 11.
  4. REM, about an hour to an hour and a half after you fall asleep p. 12.

The stages repeat all night; REM grows longer while deep sleep gets shorter p. 12. How rested you feel depends on this timing, not only on total hours p. 10.

Sleep is not one long blank. It is a loop that repeats all night.

Idea

Why snoring happens

Snoring comes from a narrowed or partly blocked airway at the back of the mouth, throat or nose: the air turns turbulent and soft tissues vibrate p. 36.

A relaxed palate, tongue and throat, allergies, sinus trouble and extra weight narrow it; alcohol or sedatives near bedtime make snoring worse p. 36.

About half of adults snore, half of them loudly and often. It rises with age, and US surveys find loud, frequent snoring more common in African Americans, Asians and Hispanics p. 36.

A snore is air squeezing through a pipe that got narrower.

Snoring without pauses still costs

Even without breathing pauses, snoring adds effort to your breathing and can lower your sleep quality p. 37.

Older adults who snored 6 to 7 nights a week, without apnea, were more than twice as likely to be extremely sleepy in the day, and snorers are more likely to be in car crashes p. 37.

Women who snored regularly were twice as likely to develop diabetes; regular snoring may also raise the risk of high blood pressure, heart failure and stroke p. 37.

Sleep apnea: the pause and the snort

In sleep apnea, breathing stops again and again p. 44:

  1. Soft tissue at the back of the throat collapses and partly or fully closes the airway p. 44.
  2. Oxygen in the blood falls p. 44.
  3. The brain wakes you just enough to reopen the airway p. 44.
  4. Breathing restarts, often with a loud snort or choking sound p. 44.

Each pause lasts 10 to 120 seconds and can come 20 to 30 times or more an hour, so people rarely get enough deep, restorative sleep p. 44.

With apnea, you can spend all night in bed and still get little deep sleep.
