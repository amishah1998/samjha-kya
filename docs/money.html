True story

Franklin's 200-year experiment

Benjamin Franklin died in 1790, leaving £1,000 each to Boston and Philadelphia (about $4,400 then), in trust to gather interest for 200 years.

By 1990, Philadelphia's fund held over $2 million; Boston's had grown by almost $5 million Franklin's bequest.

Sharma jiSo, a genius trade?
ChintuNo. The trusts lent it out, and Boston spent part at 100 years. Mostly, a very long wait.
A modest sum plus a very long wait did the heavy lifting.

In 1920, Charles Ponzi promised Boston investors 50% profit in 45 days, from trading postal reply coupons.

Tax every year shrinks what compounds

FD interest is taxed as "Income from Other Sources", and the tax depends on your income that year FD interest tax. The tax return lists interest under other sources Income Tax Dept.

Example: Rs 1 lakh at 7%, for someone paying an example 30% tax. Of Rs 7,000 interest, Rs 2,100 goes as tax, so only 4.9% stays to compound.

Over 20 years, Rs 1 lakh grows to about Rs 3.87 lakh with no tax, but only about Rs 2.60 lakh with tax paid every year.

Tax paid every year lowers the rate that compounds.
