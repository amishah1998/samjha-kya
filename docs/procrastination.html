Picture it

The ugh loop

The ugh loop has four steps:

  1. A task gives you a small "ugh".
  2. You grab the phone.
  3. The ugh vanishes, and that relief feels like a reward.
  4. Then the guilt arrives, so next time the task feels even worse.

Rewarded habits repeat, so the escape looks even better next time.

Every escape trains you to escape again.

When it is more than putting off

DoctorHow long has it felt like this?

Now beats later

PinkyThe marks matter to me. So why does the reel win every evening?
ChintuSteel's temporal motivation theory: the longer you must wait for a reward, the smaller it feels today Steel 2007. The reel pays now. The marks are 30 days off.
Sharma jiLike a building far away. Huge, but it looks small.
Yaad rakho
  • Near rewards feel big
  • Far rewards feel small, even huge ones

IntermediateCh 2: Why does "later" always win?4/19
Worked example

Thomas Delay's semester

Steel's paper uses a made-up student, Thomas Delay. His essay is set on September 15 and due December 15 Steel 2007.

  1. Going out is fun today, every day, so its pull stays high.
  2. The essay's reward is far off, so its pull starts low and climbs only as the deadline nears.
  3. The lines cross on December 3. That leaves 12 days for a 3-month essay.
The deadline does not create the work. It just makes the reward finally feel close.

True story

The long bill

A Swedish team signed up 3,525 students from 8 universities. Nine months later, 2,587 of them answered again.

Students who procrastinated more reported a bit more depression, anxiety and stress. They also slept worse and felt lonelier Neuroscience News.

AdvancedCh 3: So how do you stop?3/21
True story

The Christmas Eve report

In 1997, Peter Gollwitzer and Veronika Brandstätter asked students to write a report on how they spent Christmas Eve, within 2 days after it.

Some students also wrote down exactly when and where they would write it. 71% of them wrote it in time, against 32% of the others. It was a small study: the result rests on 36 students Gollwitzer and Brandstätter 1997.

In one small study, deciding when and where more than doubled the share who got it done.

Decide once, in advance, so the moment does the deciding.

Make the first step tiny

"Do the assignment" is huge, vague and scary, so it gets delayed. Pychyl's trick is one question: "What's the next action I need to do, if I was to get started?" Carleton

  2. Open the file.
  3. Type the title.
  4. Write one ugly line.

Steel's review agrees that small, near goals shorten the delay Steel 2007.

Make step one so small it would be silly to skip.

AdvancedCh 3: So how do you stop?7/21
The study

Audiobooks only at the gym

Katy Milkman's team studied 226 students and staff at a university gym for nine weeks. One group got gripping audiobooks that played only at the gym. That group went 51% more often than a comparison group, though the boost weakened over the weeks.

Researchers call this temptation bundling. Afterwards, 61% of participants were willing to pay to lock their audiobooks to the gym Wharton.

Desi version: that one web series only on the treadmill.

Lock a treat to the dull task, so the reward arrives now.

5 things to remember

  1. Decide when and where, in advance.
  2. Shrink the first step.
  3. Start before you feel like it.
  4. Move the phone, not your willpower.
  5. Forgive the slip, then replan.

Why does Thomas Delay's essay only win near the deadline?

Answer

Going out pays every day, but the essay's reward is far off, so its pull starts low. It wins only on December 3, when the deadline is close.
